# Electrical Maintenance & Safety Policy

## 1. Common issue categories

Total loss of power to a unit, partial power loss, exposed or damaged wiring, sparking outlets, and a distribution
board (DB) that trips repeatedly and will not reset.

## 2. Priority guidance

Exposed live wiring, sparking outlets, or any burning smell is Emergency. Total loss of power to a unit is Urgent.
A single dead outlet with all other outlets and circuits functioning normally is Routine. Any electrical issue
combined with visible water ingress is always Emergency regardless of how minor the electrical fault appears on
its own.

## 3. Qualified electrician requirement

Any work on a distribution board, or any repair requiring a Certificate of Compliance (COC) under the Electrical
Installation Regulations, may only be performed by a licensed electrician on the approved vendor panel. A COC must
be issued and filed against the ticket for any DB-level work.

## 4. Load shedding versus fault distinction

A scheduled load-shedding outage affecting the whole building or area is not a maintenance fault and should not be
logged as a ticket — direct tenants to the published load-shedding schedule instead. Only log an electrical ticket
if power loss persists after the scheduled load-shedding window has ended, or if it affects only one unit while
neighbouring units have power.

## 5. Safety instructions for tenants pending arrival

Advise tenants to switch off the affected circuit breaker if it is safe to reach, avoid touching any exposed
wiring or standing water near an outlet, and not to attempt any repair themselves.
