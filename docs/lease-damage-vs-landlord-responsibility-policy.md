# Lease Damage vs. Landlord Responsibility Policy

## 1. General principle

Marula, as landlord, is responsible for fair wear and tear and for the failure of building systems and structural
elements. Tenants are responsible for damage caused by their own negligence, misuse, or unauthorized alterations.

## 2. Examples of landlord responsibility

Geyser failure due to age or a manufacturing fault, structural plumbing or electrical infrastructure failures,
common building systems (lifts, fire safety systems, HVAC plant), and any defect present at the start of the
lease that was not disclosed.

## 3. Examples of tenant responsibility

A blocked drain caused by improper disposal (grease, foreign objects), a broken fixture resulting from misuse,
damage from unauthorized alterations or fittings installed without approval, and damage arising from a tenant's
failure to report a known issue promptly, where that delay measurably worsened the damage.

## 4. Disputed cases

Where responsibility is not clear-cut, the attending vendor's photographic assessment (see the Water Leak &
Damage Response Protocol) is the first point of reference. The Facilities Manager makes the determination; a
tenant may request an independent assessment at their own cost, refundable if the independent assessment
overturns the original determination.

## 5. Billing procedure for tenant-liable repairs

Where a repair is determined to be tenant-liable, the cost is invoiced directly to the tenant with the vendor's
itemized invoice attached — it is never silently added to a utility bill or levied without an itemized
explanation.
