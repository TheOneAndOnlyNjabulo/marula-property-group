# Fire Safety & Compliance Policy

## 1. Scope

Fire detection panels and smoke detectors, alarm systems, fire extinguishers, sprinkler systems, emergency
lighting, and fire doors.

## 2. Priority guidance

Any fault that disables fire detection or suppression coverage for part or all of a building — a dead detection
panel, a sprinkler system offline, a fire door that will not close — is Emergency by default, even though no fire
is present, because the building is left without protection until it is fixed. A single component fault with full
redundancy intact elsewhere (for example, one detector offline out of many on the same floor) is Urgent.

## 3. Statutory compliance

Fire equipment must be serviced annually in line with SANS 10400-T, and the resulting Certificate of Compliance
must be retained and made available on request to the local fire authority. Servicing tickets are Routine unless
they reveal a fault, at which point the ticket is reclassified per section 2 above.

## 4. Evacuation procedure ticket handling

A ticket generated as a result of an actual evacuation (e.g. a triggered alarm) must be logged as Emergency
regardless of the eventual root cause, since the building was without confirmed detection coverage during the
event until the system is reset and verified.

## 5. False alarm handling

A confirmed false alarm still requires the underlying detector or panel fault (if any) to be investigated and
logged as its own ticket — closing a false-alarm ticket without a root-cause check is not permitted.
