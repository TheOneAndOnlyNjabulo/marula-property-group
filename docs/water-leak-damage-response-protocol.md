# Water Leak & Damage Response Protocol

## 1. Immediate containment steps

On any reported leak, the first priority is containment: isolate the water supply at the nearest valve and, if
the leak is near any electrical outlet, panel, or equipment, treat the ticket as Emergency under the Electrical
Maintenance & Safety Policy's water-ingress rule regardless of how the leak itself would otherwise be classified.

## 2. Priority classification cross-reference

Active, ongoing flooding is Emergency per the Priority Classification Policy. A contained, slow leak that is not
actively spreading is Urgent. This document does not introduce a separate priority scale — it exists to give
containment and drying steps once a plumbing-related ticket has already been classified per that policy.

## 3. Drying and mould prevention timeline

Affected areas must have active drying (fans, dehumidifiers, or extraction) started within 24 hours of the leak
being contained, and ideally within 48 hours at the outside, since mould growth risk increases sharply beyond that
window on porous materials like carpet and drywall.

## 4. Damage assessment and photographic evidence

Before any repair or drying work begins, the attending vendor must photograph the affected area and any damaged
tenant property, since this record is required for both the building's insurance claim and any dispute over
tenant versus landlord responsibility for resulting damage.

## 5. Interaction with related policies

Responsibility for the underlying repair cost is determined per the Lease Damage vs. Landlord Responsibility
Policy. Where tenant property (furniture, stock, equipment) is damaged, the tenant is directed to the building's
insurance claims contact rather than Marula's own maintenance budget.
