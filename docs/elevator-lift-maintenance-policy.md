# Elevator/Lift Maintenance Policy

## 1. Entrapment procedure

A reported entrapment is always Emergency, with a sub-30-minute response target regardless of the hour — this
overrides the standard Emergency response window in the SLA Response & Resolution Times document. The helpdesk
agent must stay on the line with the trapped occupant (via the lift intercom or their phone) until the vendor
confirms arrival.

## 2. Out-of-service (non-entrapment) handling

A lift that is out of service but not currently trapping anyone is Urgent, not Emergency, unless the building has
only one working lift and a tenant has a documented mobility requirement, in which case it is escalated to
Emergency.

## 3. Statutory inspection requirements

Every lift requires an annual inspection under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, and its current inspection
certificate must be displayed inside the lift car. An expired certificate takes the lift out of service
immediately, regardless of whether a fault is present.

## 4. Vendor certification requirement

Only vendors certified for lift maintenance and repair under the relevant OHS Act regulations may be dispatched
for lift tickets — this is a narrower certification than general electrical or mechanical trade licensing.

## 5. Tenant communication during extended outages

If a building has only one lift and it will be out of service for more than 4 hours, all tenants in the building
must be notified proactively, not just the tenant who logged the ticket, per the Tenant Communication Standards.
