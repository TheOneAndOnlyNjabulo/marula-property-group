# Priority Classification Policy

Every maintenance ticket logged with Marula Property Group must be assigned one of three priority tiers before
dispatch. Priority drives the response and resolution targets set out in the SLA Response & Resolution Times
document — do not invent a priority level outside these three.

## 1. The three tiers

- **Emergency** — an immediate threat to life, safety, or the structural integrity of the property.
- **Urgent** — a significant loss of amenity or a risk that will become dangerous if left unattended, but with no
  immediate threat to life or safety.
- **Routine** — general wear-and-tear or a request with no safety or habitability impact.

## 2. Emergency criteria and examples

A ticket is Emergency if any of the following apply: active flooding or a burst pipe, exposed live wiring,
sparking outlets, or a burning smell, total loss of power to a unit, a suspected gas leak, an elevator entrapment,
any fault that disables fire detection or suppression coverage, or a structural failure (collapsed ceiling,
compromised load-bearing element). When in doubt between Emergency and Urgent, and a life-safety element is
present in any part of the description, classify as Emergency.

## 3. Urgent criteria and examples

A ticket is Urgent if it significantly affects habitability or business operations but is not immediately
dangerous: no hot water, a leaking geyser that is not actively flooding, a blocked drain affecting the only
bathroom in a unit, loss of air conditioning in a fully tenanted office during a heat advisory, a malfunctioning
main entry boom gate, or a bee/wasp swarm near an occupied entrance.

## 4. Routine criteria and examples

A ticket is Routine if it is cosmetic or low-impact and can wait for standard scheduling: a dripping tap, a single
dead power outlet with other outlets functioning normally, scuffed paintwork in common areas, or a routine
service request with no fault present.

## 5. Ambiguous or multi-factor tickets

A ticket that spans more than one category (e.g. a leak that has also affected an electrical outlet) is classified
at the priority of its most severe component, not averaged. A ticket that could plausibly sit at two adjacent
tiers should be classified at the higher tier — the cost of over-responding to a Routine issue is always lower
than the cost of under-responding to an Emergency. This document takes precedence over category-specific guidance
elsewhere when the two conflict on priority.
