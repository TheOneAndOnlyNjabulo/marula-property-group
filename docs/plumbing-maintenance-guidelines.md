# Plumbing Maintenance Guidelines

## 1. Common issue categories

Burst or leaking pipes, blocked drains, leaking or failed geysers, dripping taps, and blocked toilets.

## 2. Priority guidance for plumbing issues

A burst pipe or any actively flooding leak is Emergency. A leaking geyser that is not actively flooding, or a
blocked drain affecting the only bathroom in a unit, is Urgent. A dripping tap or a slow-draining (but not fully
blocked) sink is Routine. This guidance is a specific application of the Priority Classification Policy, not a
replacement for it — if a plumbing issue also threatens electrical equipment, classify per whichever component is
more severe.

## 3. Geyser compliance

Any geyser replacement or repair involving pressure control valves must comply with SANS 10254 and may only be
performed by a licensed plumber on the approved vendor panel. Unlicensed geyser work voids both the manufacturer
warranty and the building's insurance cover for water damage arising from that unit.

## 4. Water shutoff procedure

Each unit's isolation valve location is recorded in the building's maintenance log. Helpdesk agents should be able
to direct a tenant to their nearest isolation valve during an active-flooding call while a vendor is en route.

## 5. Recurring or pattern issue reporting

If the same unit or a shared riser reports plumbing issues more than twice in 90 days, the ticket must be flagged
for a full inspection rather than another point repair, since repeat call-outs on the same line usually indicate
an upstream fault.
