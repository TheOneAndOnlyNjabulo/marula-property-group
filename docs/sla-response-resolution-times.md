# SLA Response & Resolution Times

This document sets the response and resolution targets for each priority tier defined in the Priority
Classification Policy. "Response" means a technician or vendor is dispatched and en route or on-site; "resolution"
means the fault is fixed or, where a permanent fix requires parts or specialist work, safely mitigated with a
confirmed follow-up date.

## 1. How to read this matrix

Targets are measured from the moment a ticket is logged and correctly classified, not from when it is first read.
"Business hours" means weekdays 07:00–17:00, excluding South African public holidays. Emergency targets apply
24/7/365 regardless of business hours.

## 2. Emergency SLA

Respond within 1 hour of logging (2 hours after-hours, per the After-Hours & Emergency Callout Policy). Mitigate
the immediate danger within 4 hours. Fully resolve within 24 hours where parts are on hand; if parts must be
ordered, mitigation must hold until resolution and the tenant must be given a confirmed resolution date.

## 3. Urgent SLA

Respond within 4 business hours. Resolve within 2 business days.

## 4. Routine SLA

Respond within 2 business days. Resolve within 5 business days.

## 5. Category-specific adjustments

Elevator entrapments carry a sub-30-minute response target regardless of the hour, overriding the standard
Emergency response window — see the Elevator/Lift Maintenance Policy. Any fault that disables fire detection or
suppression coverage is treated as Emergency by default even if no fire is present, per the Fire Safety &
Compliance Policy.

## 6. SLA breach handling

If a response or resolution target is missed, the ticket must be escalated per the Escalation Policy before the
target lapses wherever possible, and the tenant must be proactively notified with a revised ETA rather than left
to ask for one. A missed SLA is logged against the assigned vendor for the panel performance review described in
Vendor/Contractor Onboarding & Compliance.
